# Hike Trail foundation

Hike Trail is a continuous 24-mission expedition in four acts. Nova guides the trainee and Patch supplies the early absurdity. The story moves from playful basecamp problems to a corroborated incident at Summit Beacon. Every mission includes three story frames, two to four terminal evidence objectives, an evidence-based decision, three graduated hints, a concept check, and a debrief that connects the task to real work.

The player restores a simulated mountain signal network. Missions start gently, then combine context, command output, safe change planning, and incident evidence. No mission requires real credentials, real personal data, live network probes, destructive commands, or disabling TLS checks. Addresses use reserved documentation ranges and `.test` names. Network responses are simulated.

| Act | Missions | Foundation coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The signal goes quiet | 1–6 | Authorization and CIA goals; navigation and asset awareness; reading logs; `find` and `grep`; creating notes; preserving copies; trusted backup and verified recovery. |
| Keep the gates | 7–12 | Permissions and least privilege; password managers, unique credentials, MFA and recovery; phishing and trusted verification; social engineering and visitor access; classification and privacy; basic safe script inspection. |
| Across the wire | 13–18 | IPs, prefixes and gateways; DNS; bounded connectivity tests and their limits; TCP/UDP, ports and processes; HTTP/TLS and certificate verification; trusted hashes; encoding versus encryption. |
| The last light | 19–24 | Fictional vulnerability applicability and risk; patch rollout planning; firewall change planning; log filtering and pipelines; correlated incident confirmation; authorized containment; accurate reporting; integrated recovery capstone. |

Each mission creates its own fixtures and starts in `/home/trainee`. Examples are written for the in-browser simulated shell, not for a production host. `solutions` contains executable commands for all terminal objectives. The mission decision and quiz are completed through the game interface. A terminal command must succeed and generate the relevant evidence; typing an answer in a note does not complete unrelated objectives.

## Honest limits

This is broad foundational practice, not a claim that every cybersecurity skill or certification requirement is covered. Linux filesystem ownership, process lifecycle management, package installation, real TLS diagnosis, actual firewall administration, live account recovery, real scripting execution/control flow, programming, packet capture, endpoint protection, vulnerability scanning, cloud administration, and professional incident response require further practical modules and appropriate authorization. The script mission inspects and stages a change; it does not execute scripts. Patch and firewall missions stage reviewed requests; they do not alter real services.

Quizzes reinforce concepts but do not substitute for observed task completion. Failed sign-ins or unusual events are treated as leads, not automatic evidence of an attacker. A confirmed incident is explicitly supported by correlated verified audit, approved state, and operator confirmation. Hashing is never described as encryption. Base64 supplies no confidentiality. Completion acknowledges verified recovery while retaining monitoring and unresolved root-cause investigation.

Office and Night Shift are visible future modes. Both stay locked until their own content is built, tested, and released. Completing Hike Trail establishes foundation progress; it does not manufacture unfinished content or settle future difficulty/progression policy.
